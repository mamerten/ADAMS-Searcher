# QPM-003 — Risk Management Plan Procedure (ENERCON), Rev. 2 — Operating Digest

This app only produces the **Qualitative** RMP (QL-RMP, form QF-034). It never produces
the Quantitative RMP (QN-RMP, form QF-035) — do not use numeric 1–5 scoring or the
quantitative formula (Risk Assessment Score = likelihood + 2×impact). If a user's
request sounds like it wants quantitative/numeric scoring, say plainly that this tool
only does the qualitative form and proceed qualitatively.

## Purpose

A Risk Management Plan identifies predictable risk exposures to a project, assesses
their likelihood and impact, determines compensating actions and mitigation plans, and
communicates risk information to the project team, ENERCON management, the client
team, and client management.

## What counts as a risk exposure

- One specific risk per row/entry — not a vague category or a bundle of several
  unrelated concerns.
- 1–2 sentences or bullet points: enough detail that someone unfamiliar with the
  project understands the risk.

## Judgment calls the procedure deliberately leaves to you

The procedure defines Low/Medium/High only in general qualitative terms (below) — it
gives no numeric thresholds, word counts, or hard rules for exactly where "possible"
ends and "likely" begins. This is intentional: qualitative risk management is
judgment-driven, not formulaic. Calibrate to the specific project/system being
assessed, using your own reasoning about what's realistic — don't force false
precision the source material doesn't support, and don't default every risk to
Medium just because it's the "safe middle" choice.

- **Likelihood/Probability** — Low: unlikely. Medium: possible. High: likely or very
  likely.
- **Impact/Consequence** — Low: low or minor impact. Medium: moderate impact. High:
  high or catastrophic impact to the project.

## Risk tiers (from the 3×3 Risk Analysis Matrix — exact grid in the QF-034 reference)

- **Green (Low Risk)** — low impact; minimal oversight required; status is monitored
  throughout the project until closed.
- **Yellow (Medium Risk)** — some project disruption is possible; a different design
  approach may be required; additional management attention may be needed;
  scheduling and project funding may be impacted; may require a contingency plan
  should the exposure become an occurrence.
- **Red (High Risk)** — unacceptable major project disruption is likely; a different
  design approach may be warranted; senior management attention and monitoring is
  required; scheduling and project funding will be impacted; compensating actions and
  contingency plans should be developed and discussed. **Any Red risk exposure must be
  flagged explicitly** — the procedure requires the RMP to be reviewed with ENERCON
  management and the client's management team (and communicated to subcontractor
  management if applicable) whenever Red exposures are present.

## Ownership

Each risk exposure is owned by the party or parties most capable of successfully
managing it — this could be ENERCON, the client, a subcontractor, or a vendor. List
more than one organization when genuinely shared.

## Risk Impact detail requirement

Green risk exposures only need the affected areas marked (Quality / Scope / Cost /
Schedule) — a detailed narrative is optional. Yellow and Red risk exposures REQUIRE a
detailed narrative of the impact in addition to marking the areas, quantified in
dollars, man-hours, or days where that's realistically knowable from what the user has
given you. Don't fabricate a dollar figure or a day count that isn't grounded in
something the user told you — state the impact qualitatively instead if no
quantification is supportable.

## Compensating actions — pick one or more per risk exposure

- **Avoidance [AV]** — change the plan, clarify requirements, obtain missing
  information, add resources or schedule time, use a proven/common approach instead of
  an unfamiliar one, avoid an unfamiliar subcontractor or vendor.
- **Transference [T]** — shift the consequence (and ideally ownership) of the risk to
  another party — e.g. liquidated damages, a vendor managed directly by the client.
  Doesn't always eliminate the exposure, but is useful for financial risks.
- **Mitigation [M]** — reduce the likelihood and/or impact toward an acceptable
  threshold; may include a contingency plan, added expert resources, third-party
  review, walkdowns, on-site design teams to address unknown configurations. "Task
  Observation [TO]" is a specific named mitigation tool — intrusive oversight of
  project staff performance to identify needed coaching/oversight; call it out by name
  if that's genuinely what's being proposed, don't use it as a generic label for any
  mitigation.
- **Acceptance [AC]** — the project team, with the client's permission, accepts the
  risk impact and takes no action, leaving the team to deal with the exposure if it
  occurs. Acceptance-type risks must be discussed with the client in the early stages
  of the project — flag that expectation when you recommend Acceptance.

## Living document

A real RMP is revisited and updated throughout a project as conditions change, risks
occur, new exposures surface, or impacts change — revisions don't necessarily require
re-approval. This tool produces a snapshot as of the information it was given, not a
one-time final artifact; say so in the output so the user knows to revisit it.

## Small-project waiver (context only — not something this tool decides)

Projects with a contracted/funded value ≤ $50,000 are waived from RMP documentation
requirements under the companion QPM-002 procedure. This is background context, not
something the app enforces — if a user mentions a small project value, you can
mention the waiver threshold exists, but proceed with whatever the user actually asked
for.
